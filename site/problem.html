The problem

AI-generated content is everywhere in the feed, and it looks like everything else.

Short-form video platforms reward whatever keeps you watching. AI generators can now produce that kind of content at a volume and speed no human creator can match, and it's mixed into the same feed as real footage with no visual tell.

Kinds of harm this causes

Scams

Fabricated "before/after" or celebrity-endorsement clips used to sell fake products or investment schemes.

Fake news

Synthetic footage presented as real events, spreading faster than any correction can catch up to it.

Deepfake ads

A real person's likeness used without consent to promote something they never endorsed.

These categories are described generally on purpose — this page doesn't cite made-up statistics about how common each one is. See Research for the specific, sourced numbers this project is built on.

Why this is hard to solve

Existing signals people rely on — a blurry face, a weird hand, a label in the metadata — are all disappearing. Generators keep improving, and content-credential metadata like C2PA can simply be stripped before upload. See How it works for how TruthLens approaches this instead: by looking at the actual pixels of the content itself, every time, rather than trusting a label that can be removed.

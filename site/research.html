Research

The sources this project is built on

Every number on this site traces back to one of these three sources, an evaluation run in Results, or an audit report in Audit. Nothing here is invented.

Deepfake-Eval-2024

Chandra et al. (2025), "Deepfake-Eval-2024: A Multi-Modal In-the-Wild Benchmark of Deepfakes Circulated in 2024."

Finding: on real deepfakes collected from social media, open-source detectors' AUC dropped by 50% for video, 48% for audio, and 45% for image models compared with previous lab benchmarks.

arXiv:2503.02857 →

OpenAI's AI text classifier

OpenAI's own classifier for detecting AI-written text.

Finding: it correctly identified only 26% of AI-written text, and wrongly flagged human-written text as AI 9% of the time. It was withdrawn on July 20, 2023 due to low accuracy.

openai.com →

C2PA content credentials

Reporting on Google and Adobe's content-labeling standard for AI images.

Finding: C2PA metadata used to label AI images "can easily be stripped or swapped by bad actors" — a label is only as strong as the metadata carrying it, and that metadata doesn't survive re-uploading or screenshotting.

axios.com →

The gap

Put together, these three findings describe the same gap from three different angles: detectors that look great on clean benchmarks lose a large share of their accuracy on real, in-the-wild content; classifiers get withdrawn once real-world use exposes how often they're wrong; and metadata-based labels can simply be removed before content ever reaches a feed.

TruthLens doesn't claim to close this gap — no free, local tool can. Instead it tries to be honest about it: analyze the real frames (not metadata that can be stripped), combine more than one signal, cap confidence at 95%, and say "Unsure" when the signals genuinely disagree rather than force a guess. See How it works for the mechanics, and Results for a measured accuracy number on our own small test set.
